## ⚠ THINGS TO HANDLE CAREFULLY
1. **"Vu dans: VIVA · alive · Chatelaine · Best Health"** — same 4 real Canadian outlet
   logos seen on other Olethra products this session (ArtériVie, Duréon). Chatelaine and
   Best Health are real existing publications. NEVER render these logos/mastheads into
   generated art, per this session's standing rule.
2. **CoQ10 icon** — appears as decorative iconography in the "puissance du bleu de
   méthylène" section graphic but is not confirmed as an actual labeled ingredient on the
   product pack (pack only reads "GOMMES AU BLEU DE MÉTHYLÈNE ... 99,9% PUR"). Do not claim
   CoQ10 as an ingredient in ad copy — stick to "bleu de méthylène" as the named active.
3. **"97 % des douleurs nerveuses"** — always pair with "brûlantes" / "en 4 jours" as the
   page phrases it; don't strip context to just "97% de douleur en moins" generically.
4. **Severity framing** — page never claims to cure or treat diagnosed neuropathy/nerve
   damage; it frames relief as comfort/support ("soutien", "confort"), not medical treatment.
   Keep ad copy in the same register — supportive comfort language, not "cures neuropathy."
5. Winning ad (Michael Weber) headline: "Après six ans de neuropathie, j'ai enfin trouvé un
   moyen de soulager mes douleurs nerveuses. Voici mon témoignage, sans promesse miracle." —
   this is the CLIENT'S OWN testimonial-style ad copy device (named lower-third caption +
   red background + brain scan monitors), a strong pattern to iterate on, but the "sans
   promesse miracle" (no miracle promise) framing is worth preserving in variations — it's a
   soft, credible register, not an outsized claim.
