# Fermélia™ — teardown of the 5 client-supplied winning ads

1. **Bikini/bra bathroom UGC + circle inset.** A blonde woman in bra and blue underwear, holding
   the bottle at chest height, phone-selfie bathroom setting. Two small inset circles: one showing
   a close crop of loose inner-thigh skin, one showing legs from behind. Headline overlay:
   "La peau fripée a besoin d'une barrière protectrice renforcée. Pas simplement d'eau. Pas
   d'une lotion." Below: "Fermélia™, une huile pure pour le corps. Garantie satisfait ou remboursé
   de 60 jours." — **Device: UGC body-confidence selfie + problem/solution headline, product held
   at chest height, label legible.**

2. **Diagonal arm before/after with bottle mid-frame.** Split down a diagonal line: left side
   crepey/loose underarm skin, right side smoother skin, the bottle standing upright exactly on
   the diagonal seam between the two halves, pump visible. Blue top worn in both halves (same
   woman, same framing either side). No text overlay on this one beyond the label itself. —
   **Device: diagonal (not vertical/horizontal) before/after split, product physically placed on
   the seam as the "cause" of the transformation.**

3. **Scar before/after, clinical documentary.** A vertical surgical-style scar/mark on skin, left
   panel red/raw-looking, right panel faded to near-invisible. Plain vertical split, no text, no
   product visible — pure medical-documentary proof shot. — **Device: clinical-documentary
   two-panel proof, zero styling, zero product, the proof carries the whole ad.**

4. **Arm before/after with red claim banner.** Vertical split of upper-arm skin (crepey vs
   firmer), woman in red top, home living-room setting, black banner across the bottom reading
   "C'EST LA PEAU SUCRÉE" (an intentionally blunt/native-sounding fragment, not a translated
   phrase). — **Device: lifestyle-setting before/after + a short, punchy black banner claim at
   the bottom, no product in frame.**

5. **Post-weight-loss bathroom mirror doc shot.** A woman photographing herself in a public-restroom
   mirror, visible surgical tape/dressing across her midsection (documenting a recent
   procedure/post-weight-loss body), phone raised, red arrow annotation pointing to the taped
   area. Candid, slightly clinical, unstyled bathroom (sanitary-products sign visible on the wall).
   — **Device: raw documentary "recovery/transition" UGC shot, annotation arrow, no retouching,
   speaks to the post-weight-loss-skin narrative the advertorial itself is built around.**

## What this means for the topup
Five genuinely different devices, not near-duplicates of each other — good spread already.
Repeat count: none of the 5 repeats visually, but devices #2 and #4 (both before/after with a
diagonal or vertical split) share the underlying proof mechanism, and #1 and #5 both share the
"bathroom UGC selfie" setting family. Building 20 new concepts should give heaviest weight to
#1 (UGC+headline) and #2/#4 (before/after + product or banner), since those are the ones that
put the product itself in frame and are the easiest hooks to scale, while including a handful
each of #3 (pure clinical proof) and #5 (post-weight-loss documentary) since those anchor the
advertorial's real differentiator (loose skin ≠ irreversible aging).
