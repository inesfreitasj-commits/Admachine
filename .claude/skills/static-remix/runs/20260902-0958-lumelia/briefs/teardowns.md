# Lumélia™ — teardown of the 3 client-supplied winning ads

1. **Elderly clasped hands, dark background, curiosity hook.** Close macro of two aged, spotted
   hands clasped together, dark neutral background. Headline: "Taches brunes : cette carence
   nutritionnelle en est souvent la cause !" — a curiosity/misdirection hook (implies a surprising
   overlooked cause) rather than a direct claim. — **Device: body-part macro (no face) + curiosity
   headline about an unexpected cause.**

2. **Face macro before/after, vertical split, black banner top.** Extreme close crop of a cheek/nose
   area, vertical split down the middle, left side heavily spotted, right side clear. Black banner
   across the very top: "Les taches pigmentaires s'estompent en 36 heures." — a direct, specific
   timeframe claim, no curiosity framing. — **Device: face-macro vertical split + direct timeframe
   claim banner at TOP (not bottom).**

3. **Face 3/4 profile before/after, vertical split, white banner bottom.** A wider 3/4 profile shot
   of a woman's face (eyes visible, closed), vertical split, left spotted, right clear. White banner
   below: "Taches brunes : très peu de personnes connaissent cette vieille astuce de grand-mère."
   — folk-remedy curiosity hook. — **Device: face-profile vertical split + curiosity/folk-remedy
   banner at BOTTOM.**

## What this means for the topup
Three genuinely different devices already: body-part macro + curiosity hook, face-macro +
direct-claim banner, face-profile + folk-remedy banner. All three also connect directly to
assets already available from the funnel: the advertorial's dermatoscope exam photo, its
denial device (red X over a cryotherapy tray), and four sales-page testimonials (Camille
Bernard, Céline Marchand, Véronique Caron, Philippe Garnier) not yet built as their own ads.
A strong topup should split roughly evenly across: (a) face before/after variants (both banner
positions, different body areas — face, hands, chest/décolleté, arms), (b) curiosity-hook
body-part macros, (c) testimonial quote cards using the real sales-page reviews, and (d) the
advertorial's own denial/authority devices (dermatoscope exam, red-X cryotherapy denial,
dermatologist-holding-product authority shot).
