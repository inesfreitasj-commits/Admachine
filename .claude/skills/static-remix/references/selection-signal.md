# What the client actually picks — measured, not guessed

42 ads delivered for one product (Ferméa, FR body oil). The client returned **20 chosen**.
Every chosen file was matched back to its original by perceptual signature, so this is a
record of real selections, not an impression of them.

Read this before proposing a batch. It is the closest thing to a scoreboard this skill has.

## Hit rate by bucket

| Bucket | Made | Chosen | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before/after + person holding | 4 | 3 | **75 %** |
| UGC | 3 | 2 | 67 % |
| With product | 5 | 3 | 60 % |
| Without product (symptom on a person) | 5 | 3 | 60 % |
| Iterations on winners | 6 | 3 | 50 % |
| Shelf / out of stock | 2 | 1 | 50 % |
| Surgical marking | 4 | 2 | 50 % |
| New formats | 6 | 3 | 50 % |
| **Pure native — object in a room, no text** | **3** | **0** | **0 %** |
| **"Aggressive" — a grim private moment** | **2** | **0** | **0 %** |
| **Novel formats with no person in them** | **2** | **0** | **0 %** |

## The seven findings

**1. Every ad flagged as defective was rejected — all three.** The pinch test that did not
show the skin tenting, the shelf shot with the oversized bottle, and the chat screenshot
with the French typo. Flagging honestly and flagging accurately are the same skill, and the
QC pass is not theatre. Keep flagging.

**2. One idea, one ad. Variants are waste.** Wherever two executions of the same idea were
offered, exactly one was taken and the other dropped — both out-of-stock shelves, both
iodine surgical shots, both consultation-room surgical shots, both kitchen-vs-car UGC.
Spend the budget on distinct arguments, not on variants of one. Offer a variant only when
asked for one.

**3. Reproducing an existing winner faithfully gets rejected.** The clean five-bottle
pharmacy shelf — the closest match to the client's own big winner — was cut. The version
that changed the ARGUMENT (their slot empty, "disponible uniquement en ligne") was taken.
An iteration must change what the ad claims, not just the crop and the props. The client
already runs the original; a faithful copy adds nothing to the account.

**4. A product still life needs an authority device, not a feature.** Chosen: the Trustpilot
rating card and the pack carrying the test-winner badge. Cut: the ingredient lay-down and
the "a moisturiser is not enough" comparison. In an editorial-test system (see
`creative-systems.md`) the product does not argue for itself — the verdict argues for it.
Ingredients and mechanism are claim-led furniture and they die here.

**5. Text-free still lifes do not sell. 0 for 3.** A parcel on the doormat, a bedside table,
an oiled forearm — all beautifully made, all cut. `native-realism.md` is right that polish
is the enemy, but an ad still needs something happening to somebody. Compare what WAS taken:
a note taped to a shelf, a woman holding a cardboard sign, a phone showing an article, a
selfie with the pharmacy bag on the passenger seat. Same rawness, but each contains a human
event. **Native means unpolished, not empty.**

**6. Aggressive means a public event, not a grim private moment. 0 for 2.** A woman pulling
at her own neck at the mirror, and lotion failing on a crepey arm — both cut, in the same
batch where the client explicitly asked for more aggressive angles. What was taken instead:
surgery being planned on the skin, and a woman standing in the street with a sign. Aggression
lands as something *happening* — a decision, a confrontation, a marked-up body about to be
operated on — not as private misery. Shame is not a scroll-stopper; consequence is.

**7. On shock imagery, strip every word — including the words inside the photograph.**
The two surgical ads were taken WITHOUT the overlay: one straight from `production/`, and
one hand-edited by the client to remove both the lockup AND the "24 CM" / "EXCISION"
handwritten on the skin. The picture was doing the work and the type was in the way. When
an image is a pattern interrupt, deliver a clean version alongside the composited one and
let the client choose — it costs nothing, since the raw file already exists.

## What this means for the next batch

- Lead with before/after, UGC and symptom-on-a-real-person. They convert selection.
- Every concept must contain a person, or the evidence a person left behind.
- Kill any concept whose one-line description is a noun with no verb.
- Never propose two versions of one idea; propose two ideas.
- Every "iteration" needs a different argument from the winner it iterates on.
- Deliver shock and pattern-interrupt concepts text-free as well as composited.
