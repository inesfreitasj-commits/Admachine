# Concept library — the weekly top-up machine

The user's operating model. Read this before proposing any concept list.

## The weekly shape

**30 hand-selected concepts per week**, split:

| Bucket | Count |
|---|---|
| With product | 8 |
| Without product | 8 |
| Before/after + person-holding-product | 8 |
| Iterations on winning ads (10K+ reach) | 6 |

Scale the split proportionally for smaller batches. **No concept repeats** — not within a
batch, not against previous batches. Check earlier run folders before proposing.

---

## The quality bar — what NOT to make

Three kinds of ad waste the user's money, and they are rejected on sight:

1. **Not aggressive enough.** No hard number, no strong claim, no tension. If the headline
   could belong to any brand in the category, it is dead on arrival.
2. **Generic.** Stock-feeling lifestyle imagery — smiling person on a bike, sunset jog,
   couple laughing over coffee. These convert nothing in direct response.
3. **AI-looking.** Wrong hands, floating products, plastic skin, impossible anatomy,
   nonsense text. An ad that reads as AI-generated is rejected by the audience before the
   claim is even read.

**Before proposing a concept, ask: does it lead with a number, a claim, or a visual shock?**
If not, cut it. The user would rather have 10 sharp concepts than 20 with filler.

## Mine the winners first, every time

**Always tear down the client's winning ads in depth and use them as the guide.** Not a
glance — an actual analysis: what the visual register is, what the copy formula is, what
carries the persuasion, what repeats across several winners. The repeated composition is
the proven format and gets reproduced before anything invented.

New concepts should feel like they came from the same brand as the winners.

## Concept types

Rotate across these. Each entry notes what actually makes it work.

| Type | What makes it land |
|---|---|
| **Native editorial** | Must look like a real article from a real local outlet, not an ad. Dense small text is the failure mode — composite the text in code rather than generating it |
| **UGC style** | Phone-camera imperfection: slight blur, real room, uneven light, no studio polish. Must NOT look art-directed |
| **Before & after** | Identical framing both panels, one variable changed, timestamps or day labels |
| **Testimonial / quote card** | Short quote in quotation marks, name and descriptor, product small. Reads as a person, not a brand |
| **Direct response / product hero** | Product, one enormous claim, minimal everything else |
| **Problem agitation** | The tension before the product exists. Cold palette, isolation, avoidance |
| **Mechanism / education** | Cutaway, diagram, labelled callout. Explains *why* it works |
| **Social proof / authority** | Ratings, press logos, professional endorsement |
| **Lifestyle / aspiration** | **Use sparingly** — the easiest type to make generic. Only with a hard claim attached |
| **Curiosity / pattern interrupt** | A visual with no obvious product link that stops the scroll. Often no text at all |
| **Comparison** | Product vs the old way, side by side, your column winning |
| **Unboxing / first impression** | Parcel, hands, first look. Hands are an AI-tell — check carefully |

## Proven scene ideas (client-supplied, all validated as wanted)

- **Blocked arteries** — plaque narrowing the vessel, cross-sections, cutaways
- **Poor circulation** — cold hands and feet, numb legs, heaviness. Widens the market
  beyond the primary symptom
- **UGC person holding the product** — both a man and a woman; run both
- **Partner POV** — a woman holding the product, the story being that she bought it for him
  and the result is hers too. Reaches the buyer, not just the sufferer, and sidesteps the
  male-shame barrier
- **Native images** — see the native-editorial rules
- **Simple product + ingredients card** — product, natural ingredients, one bold claim, and
  the timeframe. Straightforward, no scene
- **Medical model** — a doctor holding an anatomical model of the affected organ.
  **No text at all**, just the image. The silence is the pattern interrupt
- **X-ray / medical imaging** — X-rays, scans, angiography screens. Consistently strong
- **People at the pharmacy** — real pharmacy interior, ordinary customers
- **Pharmacy shelf price comparison** — the product beside the alternatives, prices visible
- **Cardboard sign** — a person standing in front of a hospital holding a handwritten
  cardboard sign in bold with a strong claim. Protest-sign energy; reads as real, not made

## Further angles worth testing

- **The sentinel angle** — the symptom as an early warning of something worse. Fear-led,
  and strong where the client's own page already cites the risk research
- **Prescription contrast** — the local prescription form beside the product
- **Age-stratified stat card** — the same statistic at 40 / 50 / 60 / 70
- **Then vs now** — the same organ or system at two ages
- **"Someone else noticed first"** — the partner, the doctor, the friend noticed the change
- **Seasonal hook** — tie the symptom to the current season where it genuinely fits

## What an "iteration on a winner" means

Not a new ad, and not the same ad. Recognisably the same campaign.

| Keep | Vary |
|---|---|
| Layout skeleton and where the product sits | The hero visual |
| Colour register | The stat that leads |
| Type treatment and copy structure | Background and props |
| Message angle | Crop and camera distance |

Iterations sit closest to the originals by design, but must still clear the duplicate
threshold — Meta reads near-identical creative as duplicate.

## Raw-native scene ideas

The viewer's own world, shot badly, with no product in it: a weekly pill organiser and a
blood-pressure monitor on a kitchen table; a prescription and a pile of pharmacy boxes;
bare feet or hands showing the symptom; a home remedy mid-use; a thermal or scan image
photographed off a phone screen. These carry no branding and no text and work purely on
curiosity.

## Copy rules

- **Aggressive angle and a strong claim on every concept.** Non-negotiable.
- **Put the timeframe in wherever there is text.** "en 7 jours", "en 90 secondes",
  "en 2 semaines" — a deadline converts harder than a benefit alone, and the user has asked
  for it in as many text ads as possible.
- Lead with the number wherever one exists.
- Every figure must be page-supported. Aggression lives in phrasing, never in invented data.

## Execution rules that keep ads from looking AI-made

- **Product scale** — never oversized, never the nearest object to the lens. Middle
  distance. Held products anchored to the face with a named pinch grip.
- **Hands** — the biggest AI-tell. Fingers must contact and press the surface with visible
  indentation. Normal proportions relative to the head. Check every held shot; regenerate
  any that look wrong rather than shipping them.
- **Skin and faces** — ask for natural texture, pores, asymmetry, real age. Not retouched.
- **Text** — quote every on-image line exactly; keep it short.
